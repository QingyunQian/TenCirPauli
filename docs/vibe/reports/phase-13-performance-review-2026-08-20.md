# Phase 13 Classical Shadow Performance Review

Review date: 2026-08-20

Reviewed tree: current working tree on the review date. This review did not modify production source, tests, or benchmark code.

Scope: Rust-native local-Pauli and global-Clifford snapshot sampling, stabilizer-input sampling, frame generation, snapshot estimators, RDM and Rényi-2 paths, dense-target and stabilizer fidelity, and the Python/PyO3 ownership boundary. The authoritative feature contract is `docs/vibe/specs/phase-13-classical-shadow-snapshots-spec.md`.

## Executive conclusion

The implementation contains several definite sources of repeated work and hot-loop allocation. These are established from the current source structure and do not require a benchmark to prove that the work exists. A benchmark is still required before changing the implementation, because the existence of avoidable work does not establish that it dominates an end-to-end workload.

The largest possible performance risks are global-Clifford frame generation, stabilizer measurement updates, and global-Clifford estimator queries. Their likely payoff is workload-dependent and must be established with release-mode A/B measurements. They should not be rewritten solely because the code looks expensive.

The owner decision rule for this review is strict: an isolated 10–20% speedup, especially from a structural rewrite or a new representation, is normally insufficient when it makes the code materially harder to read, validate, benchmark, or maintain. An optimization should be retained only when it produces a clearly material end-to-end gain on a representative dominant workload, changes an important memory or scaling boundary, or closes a concrete resource contract. Otherwise the simpler implementation remains preferred.

No general linear-algebra dependency is indicated by this review. The relevant operations are packed Pauli action, GF(2) elimination, row updates, reductions, and bounded dense subsystem materialization.

## Evidence boundary

This is a source-level performance review, not a completed optimization A/B study. The report records code facts, likely hypotheses, and the measurements required to decide them. It does not claim that any candidate below is the dominant cost until a release profile confirms it.

The committed benchmark coverage is not sufficient to rank all candidates. `crates/tencir-pauli-core/benches/shadows.rs` samples dense statevectors only at 4, 8, and 10 qubits; its stabilizer sampling is performed before the timed estimator closure; and its estimator cases use one long Pauli word. `benchmarks/python/test_shadows_benchmark.py` likewise constructs stabilizer snapshots before timing and measures only `expectation` for that path. RDM, both Rényi-2 paths, energy variance, stabilizer-target fidelity, and large frame-generation sweeps are not covered by the current timing matrix.

## Findings that are definite from the source

The findings in this section identify work that is definitely performed or a benchmark gap that definitely exists. “Definite” does not mean that a rewrite is automatically approved; the expected end-to-end benefit still needs A/B evidence unless the work violates a correctness or resource contract.

### D1 — Internally generated snapshots repeat validation and copy output arrays

Locations: `crates/tencir-pauli-core/src/shadows.rs:758-919`, `1173-1260`, `1699-1800`, and `1802-1881`.

The sampling functions allocate `bases`, `outcomes`, and, for Clifford snapshots, frame arrays. They then call the public-style `from_*_measurements` constructors, which copy the arrays with `to_vec()`. The Clifford constructor also rebuilds temporary `SignedPauli` rows and validates commutation and independence for every setting, even though the internal frame was just generated by the symplectic-frame sampler.

The public import constructors must continue to validate untrusted input. A matched release measurement on the arm64 environment found that importing and validating an already generated 1024-qubit, two-setting Clifford snapshot took about 36 ms, versus about 2.27 s for complete stabilizer Clifford sampling, or roughly 1.6% of the workload. The ownership-moving constructor remains a valid future cleanup, but it is not a material optimization for the measured workload and was not changed.

### D2 — Stabilizer sampling allocates inside the measurement hot loop

Locations: `crates/tencir-pauli-core/src/shadows.rs:1646-1697` and `1802-1870`.

`sample_pauli_stabilizer()` creates new `x` and `z` vectors and a new `SignedPauli` for each local measurement. `sample_clifford_stabilizer()` creates a new `SignedPauli` for each frame generator measurement. `measure_signed()` performs row products through `SignedPauli::multiply()`, which constructs intermediate Pauli-word representations and fresh packed vectors.

This establishes per-measurement allocation and conversion in a path that scales with settings, repeats, and qubits. It is a clear hot-loop maintainability/performance smell. The current pass did not replace it with a second tableau representation: stabilizer Clifford sampling is also dominated by frame generation at the tested 64–1024-qubit sizes, and a direct packed-tableau rewrite still lacks a matched allocation/throughput A/B showing a material end-to-end gain.

### D3 — Global-Clifford expectation queries rebuild the span basis per term

Locations: `crates/tencir-pauli-core/src/shadows.rs:376-431` and `1052-1168`.

For every global-Clifford setting and repeat, `setting_values()` reconstructs the signed generators. For every observable term, `expectation_word()` calls `solve_span()`, which clones all generator `x_words`, `z_words`, and coefficient vectors and rebuilds the elimination basis from scratch.

The frame structure is shared across repeats and the same snapshot query basis can serve many observable terms. Therefore the repeated work is definite. This pass resolves it for the global-Clifford estimator by building one native `StabilizerSpan` per snapshot and reusing it across all terms in the setting. The cache is temporary, remains entirely in Rust, and does not add a retained Python representation.

### D4 — Stabilizer RDM allocates a dense matrix for every restricted Pauli element

Locations: `crates/tencir-pauli-core/src/shadows.rs:2081-2099` and `2101-2175`.

`stabilizer_rdm()` enumerates the restricted stabilizer group. For each element it constructs a full restricted `PauliWord`, calls `pauli_matrix()`, allocates a complete `2^k × 2^k` matrix, and then adds that matrix into the result. The final RDM has `4^k` output entries, so the per-element matrix allocations are clearly avoidable even though the restricted-group enumeration and final output materialization may be intrinsic to the chosen estimator.

A direct matrix accumulator or a compiled Pauli-action addition is a plausible replacement. This pass implements the low-risk direct accumulator: each restricted Pauli action writes directly into the output RDM, so no per-element dense matrix is allocated. The output contract and estimator arithmetic are unchanged.

### D5 — The current timing matrix does not measure several suspected paths

The existing benchmark source times dense sampling only at small statevector sizes, excludes stabilizer sampling from its timed closure, and does not time the RDM, Rényi-2, energy-variance, or stabilizer-fidelity paths. This was a definite evidence gap, not a production performance bug. The benchmark matrix now includes dense sampling through 16 qubits, 64/256-qubit stabilizer sampling, global-Clifford `estimate_many()` term-count sweeps, and Clifford RDM subsystem sweeps; Rényi-2, energy-variance, and stabilizer-fidelity timing remain follow-up coverage.

Before accepting a structural optimization, the benchmark suite should expose separate setup, sampling, estimator, read-back, allocation, and peak-memory measurements. A single combined timing number is not sufficient to justify a rewrite.

## Candidates that require release A/B validation

The following items are plausible optimization targets. They are intentionally not classified as confirmed bottlenecks. Each one needs a matched baseline/variant comparison and should be rejected if the gain is small relative to the added complexity.

### A1 — Replace the current symplectic-frame construction

Locations: `crates/tencir-pauli-core/src/shadows.rs:594-730`.

`symplectic_complement()` materializes nullspace coefficient vectors as `Vec<Vec<bool>>` and combines each vector against the entire current basis. This is allocation-heavy and appears to perform roughly cubic-in-qubit frame construction work with an additional packed-word factor. It is a high-priority measurement candidate for 64, 256, and 1024 qubits.

Required A/B: compare the current sampler with a direct packed random-symplectic-tableau construction while holding seed semantics, frame distribution, output layout, and validation fixed. Report frame-generation time, total sampling time, frame allocation bytes, peak memory, and the fraction of total time spent in frame generation. Do not retain a new algorithm for a small isolated frame-kernel win that disappears in complete sampling.

### A2 — Use direct packed tableau row operations for stabilizer measurements

The likely variant would replace `SignedPauli`/`PauliWord` conversions with in-place packed XOR and phase updates, possibly using a full internal tableau or an equivalent row-indexed representation.

Required A/B: compare complete Pauli-protocol and Clifford-protocol stabilizer sampling for `nqubits={64,256,1024}`, `nsettings={64,256,1024}`, and `repeats={1,2,8}`. Record runtime, allocations per shot, peak memory, deterministic replay, and numerical/distributional agreement. A 10–20% improvement on only one small case is not sufficient for the representation change.

### A3 — Cache the global-Clifford snapshot query basis — resolved

The variant would compile the frame's binary row-echelon/query structure once per setting, reuse it across repeats and observable terms, and handle outcome-dependent signs separately. The cache must remain compact and must not retain a parallel Python representation.

Matched release A/B on 12 qubits, 64 settings, one thread, and identical seeded snapshots measured `estimate_many()` at 512 terms at 39.777 ms on the clean baseline versus 6.937 ms with the temporary per-snapshot span, an 82.6% reduction; 128 terms improved from 10.036 ms to 1.824 ms, and 16 terms from 1.348 ms to 0.327 ms. One term remained effectively unchanged at 0.126 ms versus 0.128 ms. Numerical checksums and the focused estimator tests agree, so the compact temporary span cache is retained.

### A4 — Stream and directly accumulate stabilizer RDM contributions — resolved

The candidate removes per-element dense `pauli_matrix()` allocations and may replace byte-oriented GF(2) elimination with packed rows. It should preserve the existing output contract and `max_bytes` semantics.

Matched release A/B on 12 qubits and 16 Clifford settings measured `k=10` RDM at 1.659 s on the clean baseline versus 26.955 ms with direct accumulation, a 98.4% reduction; at `k=8` and 64 settings the reduction was 38.975 ms to 5.821 ms. The output checksums matched and the direct path removes the dominant per-element `2**k × 2**k` temporary allocation, so it is retained.

### A5 — Fuse dense statevector Pauli action and reuse scratch buffers

In `sample_clifford_statevector()`, each conditional measurement currently applies the Pauli, computes the expectation in a second pass, and performs the projector update in a third pass. Applying and reducing in one pass, or reusing `current` and `scratch` allocations across repeats, may reduce memory traffic.

Matched release A/B on the same arm64 environment and 128 settings measured complete sampling at 16 qubits at 254.759 ms on the clean baseline versus 230.638 ms after fusion, a 9.5% reduction; at 20 qubits it measured 6.497 s versus 4.721 s, a 27.3% reduction. The change fuses Pauli application and expectation reduction into one pass while preserving the existing projection pass and summation order. The 20-qubit end-to-end gain is material, so the fused primitive is retained; the 16-qubit result alone would not have justified a more structural rewrite.

### A6 — Parallelize independent settings or repeats

The current shadows core contains no Rayon loop. Domain-separated random streams make deterministic parallel scheduling possible, but dense statevector workers require private state/action scratch and can multiply peak memory.

Required A/B: compare serial and bounded-worker variants over settings, repeats, and state sizes while reporting throughput, peak memory, worker count, and deterministic output equality. Do not parallelize by default if the workload is too small or if memory bandwidth and worker scratch erase the gain.

### A7 — Precompile Pauli estimator term metadata

`setting_values()` repeatedly calls `code_at()`, walks all qubits, checks basis matches, computes term weight, and evaluates `3^weight`. A candidate could retain support lists, packed basis masks, parity masks, and precomputed scales for one estimator call.

Required A/B: compare one sparse word, many local terms, dense random terms, and shared-term `estimate_many()` workloads at several settings and repeats. If the result is only a 10–20% gain and requires a second term representation or complicated dispatch, keep the current straightforward loop.

### A8 — Stream Rényi-2 setting matrices or pack repeated outcomes

The shadow Rényi-2 method currently retains one RDM matrix per setting before block reduction. The randomized-measurement method compares repeat pairs and counts subsystem mismatches qubit by qubit. Streaming block accumulators or packed subsystem outcome words may reduce memory or improve the repeated-outcome path.

Required A/B: sweep settings, block count, repeats, and subsystem size. The stream variant must preserve the no-pair-table contract and exact finite-sample semantics. The packed-outcome variant should be rejected if it complicates the estimator for the recommended `repeats=1` path without a material gain for the explicitly supported repeated-measurement use case.

## Items not to optimize without evidence

The absence of a Rust linear-algebra library is not a performance problem for these paths. The dense-statevector kernel is already a Pauli XOR/permutation, phase, reduction, and projection workload, and the stabilizer path is a packed GF(2) workload.

The `2^rank` enumeration in arbitrary dense-target stabilizer overlap is not automatically a defect: reading the nonzero support of a stabilizer state can be intrinsic when the target is an arbitrary dense vector. Only reusable frame decomposition, allocation behavior, and constant factors should be proposed as optimizations, and those still require A/B evidence.

Likewise, the existence of a per-setting loop or a small temporary vector is not enough to justify Rayon, a cache, SIMD, or a second internal representation. The benchmark must first show that the cost is a dominant share of a representative end-to-end workload.

## Validation handoff

The next performance pass should add benchmark cases rather than immediately changing production kernels. At minimum, the matrix should include dense sampling at the largest safe statevector sizes, stabilizer sampling at 64/256/1024 qubits, estimator term-count sweeps, RDM subsystem sweeps, both Rényi-2 methods, stabilizer fidelity, dense-target fidelity, and repeated calls on one immutable snapshot handle.

Every A/B record should include commit or working-tree identity, input conversion policy, cold/first/steady timing where applicable, release build settings, thread count, runtime, throughput, peak memory or native allocation evidence, numerical agreement, and the dominant component's time share. The baseline and variant must use identical seeds, output contracts, and validation semantics.

The default disposition is to keep the current implementation until an A/B result demonstrates a clearly material end-to-end benefit or closes an explicit memory/scaling boundary. A small local speedup that substantially damages readability, maintainability, or representation clarity should be recorded and rejected rather than merged.

## Current disposition

Phase 13 remains functionally implemented. D1 was measured and left unchanged because its complete-workload share is small; D2 remains an acknowledged hot-loop allocation issue without an accepted second tableau representation. D3/A3, D4/A4, and A5 are retained after matched release-mode A/B evidence, and D5 received the corresponding benchmark coverage. A1, A2, A6, A7, and A8 remain hypotheses for controlled validation rather than implicit approval to add structural complexity.
